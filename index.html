The problem

Young India gets its news
by accident.

Young Indians want to know what is going on. They just do not want to sit through a newspaper or a shouting match on prime-time TV to find out.

So news reaches them sideways, while scrolling. A clip on a reel. A screenshot forwarded on WhatsApp. A stranger's opinion dressed up as fact. Entertaining, unreliable, and they know it.

They finish an hour feeling informed and misinformed at the same time, and vaguely guilty about where the evening went.

2–3 hrsspent scrolling every day, mostly on Instagram and YouTube
65%of Indian Gen Z discover news incidentally, while scrolling something else

The product

The same scroll.
None of the noise.

b.rief looks and moves like the app you already open a hundred times a day. Full-screen cards, swipe up, stories at the top, follow whoever you like. Same rhythm, same entertainment.

But there is nothing else in it. No reels of strangers dancing. No rage bait engineered to keep you there. Just news, told in a way that is genuinely fun to move through.

How journalists work with us

They get the byline.
We hold the publish button.

The journalist gets

  • Their own profile, byline and following
  • An audience they own and can grow
  • A share of what their work earns

b.rief keeps

  • The verification desk every story passes through
  • The publish button, and the responsibility that comes with it
  • The correction, published as loudly as the error

Accountability is a product feature here, not a policy page. Incumbents cannot copy it easily, because copying it means accepting responsibility they have spent a decade engineering away from.

Who it is for

18 to 27, and permanently on their phone.

Students, first jobbers, exam aspirants and young professionals across metros and tier-two cities.

They already consume news, just accidentally. They want to sound informed among friends, at work, in an interview.

And for the millions preparing for competitive exams, where current affairs is an examined subject, a daily brief is not optional. It is homework they would rather enjoy.
